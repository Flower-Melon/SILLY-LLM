# Task Allocation: Wash the Lettuce and Place It on the Countertop

## 1. Subtask Decomposition

| Subtask | Description | Required Skills | Object(s) & Mass |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|------------------|
| **SubTask 1** | Wash the Lettuce | `GoToObject`, `PickupObject`, `PutObject`, `SwitchOn`, `SwitchOff` | Lettuce (0.47), Sink (0.0), Faucet (0.0) |
| **SubTask 2** | Place the Lettuce on the Countertop | `GoToObject`, `PickupObject`, `PutObject` | Lettuce (0.47), CounterTop (0.0) |

**Dependency:** SubTask 2 depends on SubTask 1 (the lettuce must be washed before it is placed on the countertop). Therefore, they must be executed **sequentially**, not in parallel.

---

## 2. Robot Capability Analysis

| Robot | Skills | Mass Capacity |
|-------|--------|---------------|
| **robot1** | GoToObject, BreakObject, SwitchOn, SwitchOff, PickupObject, PutObject, DropHandObject, ThrowObject, PushObject, PullObject | 100 |
| **robot2** | GoToObject, PickupObject, PutObject | 100 |
| **robot3** | GoToObject, BreakObject, SliceObject, SwitchOn, SwitchOff, PickupObject, PutObject, DropHandObject, ThrowObject, PushObject, PullObject | 100 |

All robots have a mass capacity of **100**, which far exceeds the heaviest object involved (Lettuce = 0.47). So **mass is not a constraint** here — allocation is driven purely by **skills**.

---

## 3. Skill Matching

### SubTask 1 — Wash the Lettuce
Required: `GoToObject`, `PickupObject`, `PutObject`, `SwitchOn`, `SwitchOff`

- **robot1**: ✅ has all five skills
- **robot2**: ❌ missing `SwitchOn`, `SwitchOff`
- **robot3**: ✅ has all five skills

→ SubTask 1 can be performed by **robot1 or robot3** individually. No team needed.

### SubTask 2 — Place the Lettuce on the Countertop
Required: `GoToObject`, `PickupObject`, `PutObject`

- **robot1**: ✅
- **robot2**: ✅
- **robot3**: ✅

→ SubTask 2 can be performed by **any robot** individually.

---

## 4. Allocation Decision (Minimum Number of Robots)

Since SubTask 2 must run **after** SubTask 1, and both subtasks can be handled by a single robot, the minimum number of robots required is **1**.

**Choose robot3** for both subtasks because:
- It possesses every skill needed for SubTask 1 (including `SwitchOn`/`SwitchOff`).
- It also possesses every skill needed for SubTask 2.
- Using one robot avoids unnecessary hand-off of the lettuce between robots.

> (robot1 would also work, but robot3 is selected as the single capable robot; robot2 is excluded because it lacks the switch skills for SubTask 1.)

---

## 5. Execution Plan

**Sequential execution using robot3 only:**

```
Step 1 (SubTask 1 – Wash the Lettuce) — robot3:
    GoToObject('Lettuce')
    PickupObject('Lettuce')
    GoToObject('Sink')
    PutObject('Lettuce', 'Sink')
    SwitchOn('Faucet')
    time.sleep(5)
    SwitchOff('Faucet')
    PickupObject('Lettuce')

Step 2 (SubTask 2 – Place Lettuce on Countertop) — robot3:
    GoToObject('CounterTop')
    PutObject('Lettuce', 'CounterTop')
```

---

## 6. Summary

| Subtask | Assigned Robot(s) | Execution Mode | Reason |
|---------|-------------------|----------------|--------|
| Wash the Lettuce | robot3 | Sequential (first) | robot3 has all required skills; mass 0.47 ≪ 100 |
| Place Lettuce on Countertop | robot3 | Sequential (after SubTask 1) | Depends on SubTask 1; robot3 has all required skills |

- **Total robots used: 1 (robot3)** — the minimum possible.
- **No teams required**, since a single robot satisfies both the skill and mass requirements for each subtask.
- **No parallelism possible**, because SubTask 2 depends on the completion of SubTask 1 (the lettuce must be washed before being placed on the countertop).
