# Task Allocation Analysis

## Task Decomposition

The task "Put two vegetables in the fridge parallely" decomposes into two independent subtasks:

**SubTask 1: Put the Lettuce in the Fridge**
- Skills Required: `GoToObject`, `PickupObject`, `GoToObject`, `OpenObject`, `PutObject`, `CloseObject`
- Unique skills needed: `GoToObject`, `PickupObject`, `OpenObject`, `PutObject`, `CloseObject`
- Object mass: Lettuce = 0.47

**SubTask 2: Put the Tomato in the Fridge**
- Skills Required: `GoToObject`, `PickupObject`, `GoToObject`, `OpenObject`, `PutObject`, `CloseObject`
- Unique skills needed: `GoToObject`, `PickupObject`, `OpenObject`, `PutObject`, `CloseObject`
- Object mass: Tomato = 0.12

## Mass Analysis

- Lettuce mass = 0.47 → all robots have mass_capacity = 100 ✅
- Tomato mass = 0.12 → all robots have mass_capacity = 100 ✅

Mass is not a constraint here since all robots have capacity 100, far exceeding both object masses.

## Allocation Decision

**SubTask 1 (Lettuce → Fridge):** Assigned to **robot1**
- robot1 possesses all required skills (`GoToObject`, `PickupObject`, `OpenObject`, `PutObject`, `CloseObject`)
- Mass capacity (100) ≫ Lettuce mass (0.47) ✅

**SubTask 2 (Tomato → Fridge):** Assigned to **robot3**
- robot3 possesses all required skills (`GoToObject`, `PickupObject`, `OpenObject`, `PutObject`, `CloseObject`)
- Mass capacity (100) ≫ Tomato mass (0.12) ✅

## Execution Mode: Parallel

The two subtasks are **independent** — neither depends on the other's completion. They involve different objects (Lettuce vs. Tomato) and can be executed simultaneously. Since we have two distinct capable robots (robot1 and robot3), we assign each subtask to a different robot and run them **in parallel**.

**Minimum robots needed: 2** (robot1 and robot3)

- robot2 and robot4 are not used because they lack the required manipulation skills (`PickupObject`, `OpenObject`, `PutObject`, `CloseObject`).
- No teams are required since robot1 and robot3 each individually satisfy all skill and mass requirements.
