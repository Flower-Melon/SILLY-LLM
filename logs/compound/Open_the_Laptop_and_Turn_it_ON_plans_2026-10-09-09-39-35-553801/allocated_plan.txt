# Task Allocation Solution: Open the Laptop and Turn it ON

## Step 1: Task Decomposition

The task decomposes into two **sequential** subtasks (the laptop must be opened before it can be switched on):

| Subtask | Action | Skills Required | Object | Object Mass |
|---------|--------|-----------------|--------|-------------|
| SubTask 1 | Open the Laptop | `GoToObject`, `OpenObject` | Laptop | 2.3 |
| SubTask 2 | Turn the Laptop ON | `GoToObject`, `SwitchOn` | Laptop | 2.3 |

## Step 2: Robot Capability Analysis

| Robot | Skills | Mass Capacity |
|-------|--------|---------------|
| robot1 | GoToObject, SwitchOn, SwitchOff | 100 |
| robot2 | GoToObject, PickupObject, PutObject | 100 |
| robot3 | GoToObject, OpenObject, CloseObject | 100 |

**Skill matching:**
- **SubTask 1 (Open Laptop)** requires `GoToObject` + `OpenObject` → only **robot3** has both skills.
- **SubTask 2 (Turn Laptop ON)** requires `GoToObject` + `SwitchOn` → only **robot1** has both skills.

**Mass check:** The Laptop's mass (2.3) is far below every robot's capacity (100), so mass is not a constraint here. No team formation is needed for mass reasons.

## Step 3: Dependency & Parallelism Analysis

- SubTask 2 **depends on** SubTask 1 (a closed laptop cannot be switched on).
- Therefore the two subtasks must run **sequentially**, not in parallel.
- Since each subtask is handled by a *different* robot, there is no shared-robot conflict — but the ordering constraint still forces serialization.

## Step 4: Allocation

- **SubTask 1 → robot3** (only robot with `OpenObject`)
- **SubTask 2 → robot1** (only robot with `SwitchOn`)

Minimum number of robots required: **2** (robot3 and robot1). robot2 is not needed.

## Step 5: Execution Plan

```
1. robot3: GoToObject('Laptop')  →  OpenObject('Laptop')     # SubTask 1
2. (wait for SubTask 1 to complete)
3. robot1: GoToObject('Laptop')  →  SwitchOn('Laptop')       # SubTask 2
```

```python
def open_and_turn_on_laptop():
    # SubTask 1: Open the Laptop  (robot3)
    GoToObject('Laptop')      # robot3
    OpenObject('Laptop')      # robot3

    # SubTask 2: Turn the Laptop ON  (robot1) — must wait for SubTask 1
    GoToObject('Laptop')      # robot1
    SwitchOn('Laptop')        # robot1
```

## Summary

| Subtask | Assigned Robot | Skills Used | Mass OK? | Order |
|---------|----------------|-------------|----------|-------|
| Open the Laptop | robot3 | GoToObject, OpenObject | ✅ (2.3 ≤ 100) | 1st |
| Turn the Laptop ON | robot1 | GoToObject, SwitchOn | ✅ (2.3 ≤ 100) | 2nd (after SubTask 1) |

**Allocation rationale:** No single robot possesses both `OpenObject` and `SwitchOn`, so the task cannot be done by one robot. Because the subtasks are dependent (open → switch on), they are executed **sequentially** across two robots. Mass capacity is not a limiting factor (all capacities = 100 ≫ 2.3), so no team formation is required. **Minimum robots used: 2 (robot1 and robot3).**
