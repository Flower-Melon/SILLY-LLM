## Step 4: Dependency & Parallelization Analysis

- SubTask 1 and SubTask 2 are **independent** (no shared objects, no ordering dependency).
- The task decomposition explicitly states they can be **parallelized**.
- Since we have 2 robots and 2 independent subtasks, we can assign one subtask per robot and run them **in parallel**.

## Step 5: Minimum Robot Allocation

- Minimum robots needed = **2** (one per independent subtask, since a single robot cannot execute both subtasks simultaneously).
- Assign **robot1 → SubTask 1 (Chill the apple)**
- Assign **robot2 → SubTask 2 (Wash the knife)**
- Execute in **parallel**.

## Final Allocation

| SubTask | Assigned Robot | Skills Match | Mass Check | Execution |

**No teams required.** Both robots possess the full skill set and ample mass capacity. The two independent subtasks are assigned to two separate robots and executed concurrently, achieving the minimum robot count (2) while maximizing parallelism.

```python
# Allocation
```
