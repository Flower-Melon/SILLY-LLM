# TASK ALLOCATION SOLUTION

## Step 1: Analyze Subtasks, Skills, and Mass Requirements

**SubTask 1: Put apple in the fridge**
- Skills Required: `GoToObject`, `PickupObject`, `OpenObject`, `PutObject`, `CloseObject`
- Object being manipulated: Apple (mass = 0.2)
- Receptacle: Fridge (mass = 0.0)

**SubTask 2: Switch off the light**
- Skills Required: `GoToObject`, `SwitchOff`
- Object being manipulated: LightSwitch (mass = 0.0)

## Step 2: Analyze Robot Capabilities

All three robots are **identical** in both skills and mass capacity:

| Robot | Skills | Mass Capacity |
|-------|--------|---------------|
| robot1 | GoToObject, OpenObject, CloseObject, BreakObject, SliceObject, SwitchOn, SwitchOff, PickupObject, PutObject, DropHandObject, ThrowObject, PushObject, PullObject | 100 |
| robot2 | (same 13 skills) | 100 |
| robot3 | (same 13 skills) | 100 |

**Observation:** All robots share the same skill set and the same mass capacity. Since all robots are equivalent, allocation is driven purely by **parallelization opportunity** and **minimum robot count**.

## Step 3: Skill & Mass Feasibility Check

**SubTask 1 (Put apple in fridge):**
- Required skills: `GoToObject`, `PickupObject`, `OpenObject`, `PutObject`, `CloseObject` → ✅ All present in every robot.
- Mass check: Apple = 0.2 ≤ 100 → ✅ Any single robot suffices.
- **No team needed.**

**SubTask 2 (Switch off light):**
- Required skills: `GoToObject`, `SwitchOff` → ✅ All present in every robot.
- Mass check: LightSwitch = 0.0 ≤ 100 → ✅ Any single robot suffices.
- **No team needed.**

## Step 4: Dependency & Parallelization Analysis

- SubTask 1 and SubTask 2 operate on **different objects** (Apple/Fridge vs. LightSwitch) and share **no resources**.
- Neither subtask depends on the other's completion.
- Therefore, they are **independent** and can be executed **in parallel**.

## Step 5: Minimum Robot Allocation

Since the two subtasks are independent and can run in parallel, we need **2 robots** (one per subtask) to achieve parallel execution. Using only 1 robot would force serialization, which is not required here.

- **SubTask 1** → assigned to **robot1** (has all required skills, mass capacity 100 ≥ 0.2)
- **SubTask 2** → assigned to **robot2** (has all required skills, mass capacity 100 ≥ 0.0)
- **robot3** → not needed (minimum robot count achieved with 2)

## Final Allocation

| SubTask | Assigned Robot | Skills Match | Mass OK | Execution |
|---------|---------------|--------------|---------|-----------|
| Put apple in fridge | robot1 | ✅ | ✅ (0.2 ≤ 100) | Parallel |
| Switch off light | robot2 | ✅ | ✅ (0.0 ≤ 100) | Parallel |

**Minimum robots used: 2** (robot1, robot2). No teams required since each subtask is fully satisfiable by a single robot. The subtasks run in parallel because they are independent and sufficient robots are available.
