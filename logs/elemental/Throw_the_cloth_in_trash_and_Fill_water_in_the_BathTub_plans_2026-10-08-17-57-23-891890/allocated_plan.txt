# Task Allocation Solution

## Step 1: Analyze Subtasks, Skills, and Mass Requirements

**SubTask 1: Throw the cloth in trash**
- Skills Required: `GoToObject`, `PickupObject`, `ThrowObject`
- Object involved in Pickup: Cloth (mass = 0.045)
- Object involved in Throw: GarbageCan (mass = 0.7)

**SubTask 2: Fill water in the BathTub**
- Skills Required: `GoToObject`, `SwitchOn`, `SwitchOff`
- Objects involved: Bathtub (mass = 0.0), Faucet (mass = 0.0)
- No PickupObject required, so mass capacity is not a constraint here.

## Step 2: Analyze Robot Capabilities

There is only **1 robot** available:
- **robot1** skills: `GoToObject`, `OpenObject`, `CloseObject`, `BreakObject`, `SliceObject`, `SwitchOn`, `SwitchOff`, `PickupObject`, `PutObject`, `DropHandObject`, `ThrowObject`, `PushObject`, `PullObject`
- **robot1** mass_capacity: 100

## Step 3: Match Skills and Mass

**SubTask 1 (Throw the cloth in trash):**
- Required skills: `GoToObject` ✓, `PickupObject` ✓, `ThrowObject` ✓ — robot1 has all of them.
- Max mass handled: Cloth (0.045) and GarbageCan (0.7) — both well within robot1's capacity of 100.
- ✅ robot1 can perform this subtask alone.

**SubTask 2 (Fill water in the BathTub):**
- Required skills: `GoToObject` ✓, `SwitchOn` ✓, `SwitchOff` ✓ — robot1 has all of them.
- Mass: Bathtub (0.0), Faucet (0.0) — no mass constraint.
- ✅ robot1 can perform this subtask alone.

## Step 4: Determine Execution Order

The two subtasks are **independent** (no shared objects, no dependency between them), so they *could* run in parallel. However, there is only **one robot** available, and a single robot cannot execute two subtasks simultaneously. Therefore, the subtasks must be **serialized** on robot1.

## Step 5: Final Allocation

| Subtask | Skills Required | Assigned To | Mass Check | Execution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Throw the cloth in trash | GoToObject, PickupObject, ThrowObject | robot1 | 0.7 ≤ 100 ✓ | Sequential (1st) |
| Fill water in the BathTub | GoToObject, SwitchOn, SwitchOff | robot1 | 0.0 ≤ 100 ✓ | Sequential (2nd) |

**Minimum number of robots used: 1 (robot1)**

No team formation is needed because robot1 possesses all required skills and sufficient mass capacity for both subtasks. Since only one robot is available, the two subtasks are executed **sequentially** rather than in parallel, even though they are logically independent.

## Step 6: Execution Code

```python
import time

def throw_cloth_in_trash():
    # SubTask 1: Throw the cloth in trash
    GoToObject('Cloth')
    PickupObject('Cloth')
    GoToObject('GarbageCan')
    ThrowObject('Cloth', 'GarbageCan')

def fill_water_in_bathtub():
    # SubTask 2: Fill water in the BathTub
    GoToObject('Bathtub')
    SwitchOn('Faucet')
    time.sleep(5)
    SwitchOff('Faucet')

# Only one robot available -> serialize the subtasks
throw_cloth_in_trash()
fill_water_in_bathtub()
```

## Summary

- **Subtasks identified:** 2 (Throw cloth in trash; Fill water in Bathtub)
- **Robots required:** 1 (robot1)
- **Teams required:** None — robot1 has all skills (`GoToObject`, `PickupObject`, `ThrowObject`, `SwitchOn`, `SwitchOff`) and mass capacity (100) far exceeds the heaviest object (GarbageCan, 0.7).
- **Execution mode:** Sequential (parallelism is not possible with a single robot, despite the subtasks being independent).
